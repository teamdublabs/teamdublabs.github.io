AI Implementation

The pilot that impressed everyone and reached no one

Constraint
A working proof-of-concept, six months old, still not in front of a customer. Nobody could say what "good enough to ship" meant, so it never shipped.
Approach
Define the failure modes that actually matter to the business, build an evaluation harness that measures them, then let the numbers decide the launch — not a meeting.
Result
A narrow slice in production behind a guardrail, with a dashboard the team trusts and a clear path to widening scope.
Workflow Automation

A day a week, spent moving the same data by hand

Constraint
Four systems already paid for, none of them talking. One person re-keying between them, and a backlog whenever they took leave.
Approach
Map it end to end before writing anything. Most of it turned out to need a well-built integration, not AI — the two steps that genuinely needed judgement got a model, the rest got plumbing.
Result
The manual step is gone, the exceptions surface to a human, and nothing breaks when one person is away.
Custom Software

Paying for a tool and working around it

Constraint
An off-the-shelf product that fit about 70% of the process. The remaining 30% had grown into spreadsheets, side channels and tribal knowledge.
Approach
Keep the parts that work, build the parts that don't, and refuse to rebuild what a licence already does well. Scope discipline is most of the job.
Result
One system that matches how the business actually operates, documented and handed over — not a dependency on us.
